Research Proposal: Security Analysis and Taxonomy of Blockchain Consensus Protocols in Modern Service Networks

Topic: Security Analysis of Blockchain (Attacks on Consensus, 51% Attacks, MEV, and Censorship)

1. Introduction and Motivation
The rapid transition from Proof-of-Work (PoW) to Proof-of-Stake (PoS) and the integration of blockchain into critical infrastructure (5G, IoT, and Cloud) has fundamentally altered the threat landscape of decentralized networks. Security is no longer just about preventing double-spending; it now involves mitigating complex economic and architectural threats like Maximal Extractable Value (MEV) and validator censorship. I have chosen this survey to investigate the robustness of modern consensus protocols against these evolving attacks. Understanding these vulnerabilities is critical for building resilient "Service Networks" where blockchain serves as the backbone for data integrity and identity management.

2. Problem Statement
Current blockchain systems face significant challenges in maintaining decentralization while ensuring security against:

Economic Exploitation: MEV strategies and front-running that undermine user trust.

Protocol Integrity: 51% and Sybil attacks that threaten ledger immutability.

Post-Quantum Vulnerabilities: The threat of quantum computing to classical consensus cryptography.

Cross-Chain Risks: Security gaps in interoperability bridges between heterogeneous networks.

3. Proposed Taxonomy
I will classify the existing literature into four primary layers to provide a "Defense-in-Depth" perspective:

Hardware-Assisted Layer: Focuses on Physical Unclonable Functions (PUF) and Trusted Execution Environments (TEE) to anchor device security (e.g., Papers #25, #26).

Consensus & Algorithmic Layer: Analyzes the mathematical resilience of PoS, PoW, and hybrid mechanisms against 51% and double-spending attacks (e.g., Papers #1, #13).

Economic & Transaction Layer: Investigates MEV, transaction ordering, and censorship mitigation (e.g., Papers #5, #9, #10).

Network & Application Layer: Covers security in 5G AKA protocols, smart contract formal verification, and cross-chain interoperability (e.g., Papers #2, #21, #30).

4. Research Methodology
This survey will follow a systematic literature review (SLR) approach. I have already identified 30 high-impact IEEE papers (2022–2026) covering these domains. The report will provide:

A comparative analysis of mitigation strategies (e.g., VDFs vs. ZKPs for MEV defense).

An evaluation of security trade-offs between scalability and decentralization.

A roadmap for future work in Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) and AI-enhanced defense.
